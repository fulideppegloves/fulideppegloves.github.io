Leather grade / Material selection

A useful grade is a measurable acceptance rule, not just an A, B or C.

Raw hides and finished leather can be graded by defects, usable area, cutting yield, thickness consistency, finish and hand feel. Commercial A/B/C labels are common, but definitions vary by supplier, so every order should carry the agreed grading criteria.

Species
Cow, goat, pig and other options
Layer
Grain leather or split leather
Physical
Thickness, softness and tear target
Surface
Scars, holes, color and usable area
Construction
Lining, cuff, reinforcement and thumb
Branding
Color, logo, labels and packing
Fulide approach: turn the leather grade into a reviewable order specification and lock it against the approved sample. A material name alone is not a protection claim.
Protection rating / Finished glove

Build to a target standard, then verify the finished construction.

EN 388 / GB 24541 Mechanical risks

Abrasion, cut, tear and puncture performance. EN 388 uses A-F for the straight-blade cut test.

EN 12477 / GB 45188 Welder's gloves

Design direction for welding heat, mechanical protection and dexterity. Type A / B describes the finished glove, not leather quality.

ANSI/ISEA 105 United States

Cut levels A1-A9, with separate abrasion and puncture ratings. These letters are not directly interchangeable with EN 388.

Target standard Buyer specified

Share the market, application, standard edition and target level so we can assess material, construction, sampling and testing.

These are standards that can be considered during OEM / ODM development. They do not mean every displayed product already carries a rating. A formal claim requires the exact model, tested construction and valid report.
